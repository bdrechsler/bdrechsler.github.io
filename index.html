W. Blake Drechsler

Hello! I'm Blake, an astronomy PhD canidate and Reber Fellow at the University of Virginia/ NRAO. My research focuses on using observations from facilities such as JWST and ALMA to study protostars, which are the stellar precursors during some of the earlest stages of star formation. Some of the key questions I am interested in are:

  • By what mechanism are these objects gaining new material and growing? Is this accretion constant or variable in time?
  • How massive are these protostars? How does their distribution of masses compare to the IMF for main sequence stars?
  • When are the conditions in the disks around protostars favorable for planet formation?

Research

My research focuses on the masses and mass assembly of the youngest, most embedded protostars. I am also interested in the circumstellar disks around these objects and when their conditions are favorable for planet formation.

Accretion in Embedded Protostars.

There are two primary accretion mechanisms that could be occuring in protostars. In magnetospheric accretion, material flows along magnetic field lines from the inner disk onto the surface of the protostar. However, if the accretion is strong enough it is possible that it can overwhelm and crush the magnetosphere, leading to boundary layer accretion where the accretion disk extends all the way to the stellar surface.

Using JWST MIRI MRS and NIRSpec observations of the Class 0 protostar L1527IRS, we detected accretion tracing atomic hydrogen lines. This indicated that accretion was likely occuring via the magnetosphere rather than a boundary layer. Additionally, analysis of the Br α luminosity has indicated that the accretion rate is too low to explain the current estimated mass of L1527IRS assuming a constant accretion rate. This suggets that the accretion must of been higher at some point in the sources history, supporting theories that protostellar accretion is non-steady rather than smooth.

Modeling Protostellar masses

The mass of a protostar is its single most defining characteristic. The stellar mass influences many other aspects of the protostellar system, from the accretion rate of the central object to the mass and gravitationly stability of the circumstellar disk. I am using full radiative transfer modeling to model the Keplerian rotation of the gas disk to obtain the most robust protostallar mass estimates possible.

I work on modeling the C18O data from the Early Planet Formation in Embedded Disks (eDisk) survey. These ALMA observations provide the largest sample of high resolution observations of embedded protostars to date and thus are the best available data to use for this modeling.
